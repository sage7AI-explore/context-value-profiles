LLM agents must fit tool schemas, documents, examples, notes and history into a token budget. The heuristics that choose
what to keep, such as relevance scores, coverage objectives, compression and fixed priorities, do not measure how much
each kind of context changes task success. We introduce marginal context value (MCV) profiles: per model and task family,
the expected success drop when a context type is removed, the value of its short variant, and pairwise type
interactions. Each is estimated once by counterfactual ablation with bootstrap intervals and a length-neutral control. A
CP-SAT compiler selects blocks under the budget, with dependencies and variants, in milliseconds and without model calls.
In a pre-registered, fully local evaluation on Qwen3-4B-Instruct (multi-hop QA over HotpotQA, BFCL tool calling,
long-history recall) against six baselines including LLMLingua-2 and a coverage selector, the primary hypothesis failed.
MCV did not exceed embedding relevance in area under the success--budget curve in any family, and lost by
\RHOneHistoryDiff{} on history, where its block ranker favored recent over relevant sessions. The efficiency hypothesis
held in two of three families: at an 8k budget MCV sent \RTokCutTool fewer prompt tokens on tool calling and
\RTokCutHistory fewer on history with non-inferior success. Interaction terms had no effect. The profiles showed that
worked examples carried near-zero value in every family, and Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B profiles agreed ($\rho=\RRho$);
a transferred profile matched a natively measured one on Qwen3-8B. We release code, data adapters and logs of every
model call.
